About BalancED

A machine learning-based system for predicting academic stress levels in university students.

Project overview

BalancED is a web application that helps university students understand their academic stress. Students answer a short questionnaire about their workload and how they feel, and a machine learning model estimates their stress level as Normal, Mild, Moderate, or High Risk. The dashboard then offers a short note for the day with a small activity that suits that level, and shows how to reach the university's counseling service. A history page shows how a student's check-ins change over time.

BalancED supports early awareness. It is not a medical diagnosis and does not replace professional advice.

Motivation

University students often face pressure from heavy course loads, tight schedules, and overlapping exams, and many do not have a simple way to see how their workload affects them. Digital wellbeing apps such as Headspace, Calm, Woebot, and MindDoc are mostly designed for the general public (Calm, n.d.; Headspace, n.d.; MindDoc, n.d.; Woebot Health, n.d.), so they rarely look at academic factors like study time or course load. BalancED focuses on university students and combines academic information with questionnaire answers.

In the UAE, official sources recognize mental health as an important part of overall wellbeing and describe efforts to address it and reduce stigma (UAE Government, 2024). The team also sees this project as supporting the UAE Vision 2031 focus on wellbeing and on using technology to support society.

How it works

  1. Create an account and complete your profile. Name, date of birth, major, year of enrollment, gender, courses taken, and average daily study hours. Height, weight, and chronic illnesses are optional.
  2. Answer the questionnaire. Short statements about workload, concentration, worry, and physical symptoms, each rated from 1 to 5. Every answer starts on the middle option.
  3. Get your stress level. A Random Forest model (Breiman, 2001), built with scikit-learn (Pedregosa et al., 2011) and trained on a student survey dataset, estimates a score from 0 to 100, which is shown as one of four levels.
  4. Use your dashboard. See your level on the gauge, read a note of the day with a routine activity for your level, and find the counseling service's contact details. Students who choose to can also get an optional AI-written message, shown in its own card.
  5. Check in weekly and look back. The dashboard suggests a new check-in once a week (this can be switched off), and the history page charts your scores over time. Any check-in can be deleted.

Privacy and limits

Your results are an estimate from a model trained on survey data, so they may not fit every student. If you are struggling, please talk to someone you trust or to the counseling service.

Account details are stored in the application's database, and passwords are stored in scrambled (hashed) form, never as plain text. The AI-written message ("From your last check-in") is optional and off by default. A student can turn it on or off in their profile, and it is always labeled as AI-generated. No email address is collected.
